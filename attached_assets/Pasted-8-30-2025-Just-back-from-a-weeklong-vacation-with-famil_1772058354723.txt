Just wanted to share this post from David Attenborough:
Once, waking up in nature early in the morning, I noticed something surprising. Several dozen ants had fallen into a five-liter bottle of water that had been left open the night before. They waved chaotically in the transparent water, as if each one was fighting for its life.
At first, it seemed to me that they were drowning each other, saving themselves at the cost of the death of others.
This thought made me repulsed, and I turned away, deciding not to intervene.
However, after two hours, curiosity got the better of me, and I looked in the bottle again.
My astonishment knew no limits: the ants were alive! Furthermore, they had formed a true living island, a pyramid, in which some were supported by others, staying afloat like an entire colony.
I held my breath and began to observe. Those at the bottom were actually submerged in the water, but not forever. After a while, they were replaced by ants from the upper layer, which voluntarily descended. Those who were tired went up, without hurrying, without pushing the others.
Nobody tried to save themselves first. On the contrary, each one made an effort to go where it was most difficult. This coordinated system of mutual aid touched me to my core.
I couldn't resist. I found a spoon that easily passed through the neck of the bottle and carefully inserted it. Seeing salvation, the ants began to come out one by one, without generating even a drop of panic.
Everything was going well, until one of them, weakened, slipped back into the water, without reaching the edge.
And then something happened that I will remember all my life.
The last ant, almost outside, suddenly turned back. He came down, as if to say: "Hold on, brother, I won't leave you!"
She dove into the water, clinging tightly to the drowning one, but she couldn't pull him out on her own. I couldn't resist, I brought the spoon closer, and then they both came out, alive, together.
This episode moved me more than any movie or book about friendship and sacrifice. I felt a storm of emotions: first, condemnation, for having taken the ants for insensitive beings; then, amazement at his resistance; admiration for his discipline and brave sacrifice... And in the end, shame.
Shame on humans. For us. Because of indifference, because of how we lose each other in pursuit of benefits, because of how rare it is that someone comes back to save the weak. We build walls, instead of creating living bridges.
If ants, small creatures, are capable of such coordination and selflessness, why are we humans so often deaf to the suffering of others?
That day I understood one thing: true strength is in unity. And if someone still doesn't know how to live correctly, let them learn from the ants.

भीम ने वनवासी हिडिम्बा से विवाह किया।
श्री कृष्ण दूध का व्यवसाय करने वालों के परिवार से थे,
उनके भाई बलराम खेती करते थे, हमेशा हल साथ रखते थे।
यादव क्षत्रिय रहे हैं, कई प्रान्तों पर शासन किया और श्री कृष्ण सबके पूजनीय हैं, गीता जैसा ग्रन्थ विश्व को दिया।

It is said in the texts that 80% of the fighting male population of the civilization was wiped out in the eighteen days Mahabharata war.
Sanjay, at the end of the war went to the spot where the greatest war took place; Kurukshetra.
He looked around and wondered if the war really happened, if the ground beneath him had soaked all that blood, if the great Pandavas and Krishna stood where he stood.
“You will never know the truth about that!” said an aging soft voice.
Sanjay turned around to find an Old man in saffron robes appearing out of a column of dust.
“I know you are here to find out about the Kurukshetra war, but *you cannot know about that war till you know what the real war is about*.” the Old man said enigmatically.
“What do you mean?”
*The Mahabharata is an Epic, a ballad, perhaps a reality, but definitely a philosophy*.
The Old man smiled luring Sanjay into more questions.
“Can you tell me what the philosophy is then?” Sanjay requested.
Sure, began the Old man.
*The Pandavas are nothing but your five senses*,
Sight,
Smell,
Taste,
Touch and
Sound...,
and do you know what the *Kauravas* are? he asked narrowing his eyes.
*The Kauravas are the hundred vices that attack your senses everyday but you can fight them*... and do you know how?
Sanjay shook his head again.
“When Krishna rides your chariot!”
The Old man smiled brighter and Sanjay gasped at that gem of insight.
*Krishna is your inner voice, your soul, your guiding light and if you let your life in his hands you have nothing to worry*.
Sanjay was stupefied but came around quickly with another question.
“Then *why are Dronacharya and Bhishma fighting for the Kauravas, if they are vices*?”
The Old man nodded, sadder for the question.
It just means that as you grow up, your perception of your elders change. *The elders who you thought were perfect in your growing up years are not all that perfect. They have faults. And one day you will have to decide if they are for your good or your bad.  Then you may also realize that you may have to fight them for the good. It is the hardest part of growing up and that is why the Geeta is important*.
Sanjay slumped down on the ground, not because he was tired but because he could understand and was struck by  the enormity of it all.
*What about Karna*? he whispered.
“Ah!” said the Old man. “You have saved the best for last. *Karna is the brother to your senses, he is desire, he is a part of you but stands with the vices. He feels wronged and makes excuses for being with the vices as your desire does all the time.*
*Does your desire not give you excuses to embrace vices*?”
Sanjay nodded silently. He looked at the ground, consumed with a million thoughts, trying to put everything together and then when he looked up the Old man was gone .... disappeared in the column of dust .... leaving behind the great philosophy of Life!

Both “Ordinary Dharma” & “Special Dharma” are “ACTIVE Dharma”, action oriented as in “Righteous-Karma” While “PASSIVE Dharma” as prescribed in Vedanta is knowledge oriented. One can do action on behalf of others but each person has to gain the knowledge through individual effort. Righteous action is the stepping-stone for “Self Knowledge.”
Thus, Active Dharma is prescribed in the form of action for the welfare of the human society as a whole. Economic development and making the world a better place to live( STANDARD OF LIVING) is the objective of the Vedas, while prescribing the Active Dharma.
Passive Dharma is prescribed in the form of gaining knowledge for enabling the individual to improve the QUALITY OF HIS/HER LIFE and enable Joyful Living.
Passive Dharma can be attempted only when one practices Active Dharma for a length of time until his mind is matured enough to understand the teaching of Vedanta.                                                                                                              Active Dharma deals with material pursuit and Passive Dharma deals with spiritual pursuit.                                                                   The result of such understanding is Joyful Living.                                                                         Source; Spiritual Library

“Ordinary Dharma” is eternal in nature. It remains same for all people, at all places and at all times. Examples of “Ordinary Dharma” are, ‘do not steal’ or ‘do not hurt’. Ordinary Dharma is inherently known to all the human beings even without any teaching. An easy way to identify an Ordinary Dharma is to check one’s expectation from others with respect to general behavior. All others in the world expect the very same behavior from us.

“Special Dharma” is that Dharma which changes from person-to-person, place-to-place and time-to-time.
Example: Everyone should work to their best of their ability in their chosen profession. What is best varies person-to-person, place-to-place and time-to-time.                                                          When there is a contradiction between Ordinary Dharma and Special Dharma, the latter prevails.
Example: A police officer should obey the orders of his superior and shoot in an encounter, violating the Ordinary Dharma of non-violence

Sharing from Mr Vijaya Kumar’s ..                         “A message which came to me about a first hand experience..not a media report..
The following is a journal of my experience on 9th May 2021.    I am comparing my personal experience to what we read in the press.  It is quite long (about 1830 words).  Please do share with others and please do comment.
A close relative passed away on Friday, 7th May 2021.  If we had to believe media reports, it would have taken us more than 36 hours to complete the cremation process.  But what was my experience?
I left home in Girinagar (Bangalore) at 7.45 AM in the morning of the 8th.  Went to MS Ramaiah Hospital.  Completed the formalities.  Enabled the transfer by ambulance to the crematorium at Chamarajpet.  Completed the formalities.  Dropped my relative in BTM layout and then was back in Girinagar by 2.15 PM.  6.5 hours.
Now, what happened in the hospital?
If we have to believe the media, the hospitals are hell bent in making the best out of the pandemic.  They are out to clear out your wallet.  There are memes which say that if you die at home, you just die.  But if you die in the hospital, you not only die and lose all your wealth.
MS Ramaiah Hospital did not charge one single paise for the treatment.  I repeat, not a single paise for the treatment.
We see the media showing photos of lots of people thronging the area with no concern for social distancing.  However, MS Ramaiah Hospital was the exact opposite.  I had taken a complete PPE kit to wear as a precaution, but there was absolutely no necessity for me to wear it. 
Now about the process for day.  Not one person was able to give me the entire process for the day.  But each person knew exactly what he had to do and he knew what the next process was.  Each person was able to guide me exactly to the next place.
If the media is to believed, then patients run “from pillar to post”. No, I went from one post to another post after getting proper directions after being given the right document in my hand.
I was given an initial slip and asked to go the ward.  I was asked to write my name and number on the slip (take a copy on the phone) and give the slip at the entrance.  There were a number of people waiting there.  I was told to maintain social distance and await my turn.  There was a large open area for us to wait.
After about half an hour, I received a call from inside.  After confirming my identity and the identity of the deceased, I was told that all the personal belongings (of the deceased) will be delivered to me in 10 minutes and I was asked to come to the entrance of the building.  They also told me that the death certificate will also be given.
Shortly thereafter someone came out and called my name as well as the name of the deceased. They handed over the items.  Another lady came running towards me and said “(the) ornaments worn by her has also been packed and put in the bag.  Please check”
(PS:  As I am finalizing this (10th May 2021), I received a call from my relative to confirm that all the ornaments were indeed received intact along with the money, mobile etc which were in the bag)
They called me inside and gave me the death certificate.  We then discovered that there was an error in the address.  I went back and told them.  Their immediate response was “Please get it rectified today itself”.  One person then walked 500 metres with me (with the full PPE kit on) in the hot sun and showed me the record room and gave me the name of the specific person whom I should meet to get this rectified.
When I met that lady, she immediately gave me an application form, took a copy of the Adhar card, told me the process.  She then gave her name and telephone number and said I could call her if the work is not done in the next couple of days.
Will the media report this?
Next, I needed four photocopies of the death certificate.  All shops were closed.  There was no way I could get photocopies done.  After running around for half an hour, I came back to the billing section in the hospital.  I asked one of the subordinate staff.  His response was stunning.  He said:  “Sir, if you offer me any money, I won’t do any work”.  He then took the certificate, got the photocopies done.  He sat me down and slowly said: “You may not be able to concentrate, but please look at this.  Here is the original and here are 4 copies.  Please take this carefully as you will need it for the next process”.
Is it that I had some VIP connections or hospital connections?  No. 
Is it that only I received this treatment?  No, there were lots of people there and everyone received the same treatment.  Whether it be admissions, discharges or release of the dead.  Everything was happening. In proper order.  Not one person was complaining.  There was no shouting.  No complaints of people jumping the queue because of influence.  Nothing.
I was overwhelmed by the help that I received.  On my own volition I tried to offer some money to the lower-level staff.  EVERY SINGLE PERSON REFUSED IT EVEN THOUGH THEY COULD SEE THAT I HAD COUPLE OF Rs. 500/- RUPEE NOTES IN MY HAND!!
Most of them were in a full PPE overalls and in places that were hot and humid.  Either in the open (under the sun) or in the entrance area where there was not much ventilation and too few fans!!  And they were working with a dedication like the possessed!!
Will the media report this?
Finally, I came to the morgue with a lot of trepidation as our experience of morgues is what we see in the movies.  I was received in a well-appointed office and made to sit in a queue.  When my turn came, they entered the details in a register and then sent me to the basement.  As the ambulance was ready, the stretcher was brought.  I was given enough time to identify.  They asked me multiple times whether I was satisfied with the process of identification.
Our family wants to convey our heartfelt gratitude to the management and staff of MS Ramaiah Hospital.  We would like to reach out to the management and see if we can be of any help to them.
Now about the crematorium.  If we were to believe the media, bodies are mass cremated.  No respect is given to the bodies.  Bodies are strewn around.  No process for identification or giving a decent final rite.
What was my experience?
We arrived around the time the crematorium was opening at 12.30 pm.  The crematorium is closed until then to prepare it for the day.  (Media will report it as Crematoriums are shut down).  We were just two of us from the family and were kind of dazed.  Not sure what to do.  We had booked our slot and we were in the queue.
A number of pyres were set up ready for the day.  Firewood was stocked in front of each pyre and ambulances were in a queue.  There were about 50-60 pyres ready though I can’t be sure about it.    Already about 10 of them were burning in a make shift area.
Suddenly, I heard someone telling: “Don’t chatter while doing work.  Chant ‘Narayana….Narayana’.”  Four people (including the driver and attendant of the ambulance) were bringing her to the pyre.  I still can’t recollect who the other two were!!  They then held our hands helped us to view one last time and then let the priest start the truncated ritual.
10 minutes later we were out of the place after completing the formalities.  We were given a slip in the hand to be brought the next day.  We were repeatedly told to keep the slip safely.
We paid the ambulance driver and the priest who coordinated in the crematorium and left.
One may argue that our case is an outlier and that we were lucky!!  While I have demonstrated that this is not an outlier, I want to ask whether we ask the same question when we read the stories in the press? Do we ever think that those examples published or televised could be outliers?
Recently, there was an appeal by a group of senior mental health care professionals led by Dr. B N Gangadhar. 
They said that “(the) media has great power to inform, educate and most importantly, infuse hope in the people…..Therefore, we request our friends in the media to show restraint…..”
Subsequently there was a video circulated by Prof Suresh Bada Math, Professor of Psychiatry at NIMHANS.   He talked of media’s responsibility to instill hope.  He talked of the ill effects of panic created by the media.  He gave the example of a journalist himself getting affected by the panic created by his profession.  He talked of the importance of the press reporting with the right statistics.  He even talked of attempts by patients to attempt suicide in the light of the panic.  Most importantly he said that the panic (created by the media) is spreading faster than the pandemic. 
He advised the press to talk to the people who have recovered and increase the resilience in the population.  He exhorted them to infuse hope.
While Dr Gangadhar and others focused on news production, I want to focus on news consumption.
Why does the media behave in the way they do?  The classic definition of news is “If a dog bites a man, then it is not news.  But if a man bites a dog, then it is news”.
Once during my high school days, I came very late from school.  After the customary punishment, my journalist father sat me down and said: “Look.  I will never publish a story that says ‘All school children returned home safely from school today in Bangalore’.  I will only publish a story which says ‘One boy was abducted / met with an accident / killed while returning from school’”
The media is trained to look at outliers.  However, I believe that they don’t understand the normal distribution curve, population and sample size.  And it is because of this that they represent the outlier to be the normal.
However, general readers intuitively believe that the media understands the theory of sampling and what they publish is truly a representative sample of what is happening to the general population.
This is the root cause of the problem.
While production of news requires a lot of common sense, I think consumption of news requires even more common sense.
I hope we can teach our kids how to consume news.  But before that, we should learn the art of consuming news ourselves”
